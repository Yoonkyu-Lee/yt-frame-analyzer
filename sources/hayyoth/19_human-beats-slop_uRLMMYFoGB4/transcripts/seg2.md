- He argues that AI generates content fast and cheaply, flooding the web with "clones", but that cheap content kills attention and trust, so staying human (pure human-to-human connection) is what matters. He offers his own channel as "living proof": a Zero-AI channel that went from nearly nothing to 611K views / ~10.8K subscribers in about a month, with a live subscriber counter ticking up on screen (10,841 -> 10,845 within the segment).
- He uses YouTube Studio as evidence: dashboard, analytics graph (sharp jump in daily views), Advanced mode "Live subscriber count", and the Community comments page to show he reads viewers' comments ("i read every comment , thousand comment"). Comments show viewers asking about his keyboard, asking for web-dev videos, and one arguing plain HTML/CSS sites impress recruiters; he treats these as human interaction.
- The yellow "2 week" sticky is a personal routine rather than a slogan: every day learn something new, build something from scratch, talk to the community, think of new ideas, and be better than 2 weeks ago. He then adds "So, who outperforming ?" answering the pink sticky's question "Is a vibe coder really outperforming raw human skill?", then deletes the note and replaces it with the longer "AI creates things fast and cheap" argument.
- Method: notes are typed, edited, bolded and rearranged live in macOS Stickies while he talks; he drag-selects lines with the mouse as he reads them (selection as pointer), and rewrites the pink note ("The myth:" header) to frame the opposing view as a myth. The last line, "just pure Human - pure Video beat AI", is the thesis; he then reaches for the menu-bar recording control to stop.
- Philosophy: not anti-tech (used AI from day one), but an extreme "Zero AI" experiment to prove that skill, daily consistency and audience trust outperform "press a button, get everything." Simple, hand-made, daily practice and human connection are valued over speed and volume.
