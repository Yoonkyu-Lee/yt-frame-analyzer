## Observations

What the speaker argues: the team's volume is huge (about 11k merged PRs in October, 23,370 over jun-oct, growth each month), release cadence is high (41 desktop releases aug-oct, "about once a day"), the contributor base grew (15 people in jun to 227 in oct, mostly casual/drive-by contributors with 1 to 9 PRs, plus a small core), and yet the revert rate fell from 0.98% to a steady ~0.47-0.48%, and app performance improved on many metrics since launch ("may" vs "now"). She then asks "should i build a software factory" and answers (start of the pyramid slide) that before automating everything, whenever the agent needs correcting, fix the codebase first, starting with a lint rule against anti-patterns.

Backed by on-screen numbers: PR counts per month, release counts and median hours between releases, contributor counts by PR-count bucket, revert rate per month, the full may/now performance grid. Bare assertions: "a wild number", "pretty good for an Electron app", "much faster than many other agent UIs out there" (no comparison shown), "isn't this going to be chaos / slop" answered only by the revert-rate chart. Notes on what the charts do not show: revert rate is "% of commits reverted" (the caption says "PRs that get reverted ... relative basis"); the grid also shows regressions (long chat cold +8%, resize +130%, idle memory +12%, idle CPU +100%, JS+CSS at startup +285%) that the speech does not mention, only "startup, typing, opening new chats, switching chats" are called faster. The 19:12 caption says "much faster than many other agent UIs" without data.
